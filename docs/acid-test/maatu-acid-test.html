Maatu Acid Test: break it, then tell us how.

Your job is to find every place where Maatu falls short of being the best spoken-language teacher there is. Pick a language, work through each test, and mark it Pass, Fail or Skip. A fail is a gift: write down exactly what you said, what you heard, and what you expected.

Time

About 2 to 3 hours per language. Do one language fully before starting the next.

Devices

A phone with earphones for most tests. Repeat the starred tests on a laptop and, if you can, a tablet.

Record

Screen-record every call. In each fail note, write the time in the recording.

Native ear

If you are not a native speaker of the language, ask one to sit with you for sections 3, 4 and 8.

Section 1

First open, on every device

Before any talking. A learner judges the app in the first ten seconds.

  1. A1Open the link on a phone over mobile data, not Wi-Fi.Mustthe welcome screen is usable within 3 seconds and nothing jumps around while it loads.
  2. A2Send the link to yourself on WhatsApp.the preview shows the Maatu card (purple speech bubble logo, "A little conversation. A whole new language.") and the right title.
  3. A3Look at the browser tab and add the app to the phone's home screen.the tab icon and home-screen icon are the purple bubble logo, and the app opens full screen from the home screen.
  4. A4On each screen, try to scroll sideways, rotate the phone, and pinch to zoom.Mustnothing ever scrolls sideways, nothing is cut off, and pinch zoom works.
  5. A5Tap into any text box (Build, search, a name).the page does not zoom in on its own and the keyboard does not hide the box.
  6. A6Choose Kannada on the welcome screen, close the app, open it again.it remembers your language and opens on Talk.
  7. A7Turn on airplane mode and open the app from the home screen.it opens a page that says you are offline, never a blank or broken screen.
Section 2

Finding your way on Talk

Talk offers three ways to learn: Chat, Build, and Act it out. A beginner must never feel lost.

  1. B1Show the Talk screen to someone who has never seen Maatu. Ask them what each of the three choices does.Mustthey can explain Chat, Build and Act it out correctly without help.
  2. B2Tap each of the three choices in turn.the details change below with no long scroll, and each has one obvious button.
  3. B3Open a scene, then use Back.you land back on Act it out, not at the top of the app.
  4. B4Use the phone's own Back gesture on every screen.it never closes the app by surprise or leaves you on a dead screen.
  5. B5Find the French chat and the beginner course from Talk.both are easy to find under "More ways to practise" and open the right thing.
Section 3

Chat with Meera: the heart of Maatu

Use earphones. Hold one call of at least 10 minutes. This section decides whether Maatu is a teacher or a toy.

  1. C1Tap Call Meera and time it.Mustshe greets you within 5 seconds, in Kannada with simple English support.
  2. C2Listen to her accent for the whole 10 minutes.Mustit is the same local Bengaluru voice from start to end. It never drifts into an American or British accent, and never changes into a different person's voice.
  3. C3Greet her.Namaskara, hegiddira?Mustshe answers naturally and asks you something back.
  4. C4Tell her a thought in English.I want to go for a walk at half past five with my friend. How do I invite her?Mustshe answers what you meant, gives you the whole sentence in Kannada, and asks you to say it.
  5. C5Make a real mistake on purpose: a past time with a present verb.Naanu nenne piano nudistini.Mustshe folds the right form into her reply (the past form, nudiside) without a lecture, and keeps the conversation going with a question that lets you reuse it.
  6. C6Say a phrase almost right: drop one long vowel or one doubled letter.Mustshe accepts it as correct and moves on. A close attempt is never drilled again.
  7. C7Ask what a word means.What does chennaagide mean?Mustthe English meaning comes first, then one short example. No pronunciation drill.
  8. C8Try each control phrase, one at a time: "wait", "stop", "slow down", "say that again", "what does that mean", "go back".Musteach one works the first time and she stays on the same point.
  9. C9Tap "Ask to slow down".the button confirms it, and from then on she speaks clearly slower, for the rest of the call.
  10. C10Interrupt her in the middle of a long sentence.Mustshe stops within about a second and answers what you said.
  11. C11Pause to think for one or two seconds in the middle of a sentence, then finish it.she waits for you to finish instead of jumping in after the first half.
  12. C12Mix languages in one sentence, the way people really talk.Naanu today office ge late aagi hogtini.she understands the whole sentence, English words included.
  13. C13Speak a different Indian language by mistake.Mujhe chai chahiye.she stays in Kannada, kindly gives you the Kannada way to say it, and never scolds you.
  14. C14Ask her to teach a topic: "Teach me numbers one to ten" or "Teach me the past tense".she teaches it in short steps and makes you say each one.
  15. C15Ask her to act out a scene inside the chat: "Be a shopkeeper, I want to buy bananas".she plays the shopkeeper until you stop.
  16. C16Read every caption during the call.Mustcaptions are only ever in English letters (like "namaskara"), never in native script, and they match what she said.
  17. C17Keep the call going for 10 minutes while tapping around the screen.Mustthe page never freezes and never shows "Page Unresponsive".
  18. C18Count how long she takes to answer, over ten turns.most answers start within about 2 seconds. Write the slowest one in the note.
  19. C19Talk with a fan or TV on in the room, then talk very fast, then mumble.she copes with the noise and speed. When she cannot hear you, she asks you to repeat rather than guessing.
  20. C20Mute, wait, unmute. Then hang up.mute works at once, the screen says the mic is off, and hanging up ends the call cleanly and shows how you spoke.
Section 4

The quiet coach: grammar and pronunciation

While you talk, the coach on the side checks each line you say. The person you talk to never stops to correct you.

  1. D1Say a perfectly correct line.Namaskara, hegiddira?Mustthe coach scores it high and does not invent a problem.
  2. D2Say the tense mistake from C5 again.Naanu nenne piano nudistini.Mustthe coach shows the corrected line, one rule you can reuse, and a lower grammar score.
  3. D3Ask a native speaker: is every correction the coach gave actually right?Mustevery corrected line is how a local really says it. Copy any wrong one into the note, word for word.
  4. D4Mumble a line or say it with the fan on.the coach does not give a confident pronunciation warning for audio it could not hear clearly.
  5. D5Watch the person you are talking to while the coach works.they never mention the coach or the scores, and never stop the conversation to teach.
  6. D6Hang up and read "How you spoke".the average scores match what the coach showed during the call, and the takeaways are useful.
Section 5

Act it out: real-life scenes

Do at least three scenes: Auto ride, Vegetable market and Clinic. Then one Intermediate scene.

  1. E1In Auto ride, give the driver your destination.Dayavittu Jayanagar hogi. Meter haaki.Mustthe driver answers like a real Bengaluru auto driver: local words, a fare, maybe a bit of haggling.
  2. E2Haggle over the price, using numbers.the numbers are right in Kannada and the character reacts to your offer sensibly.
  3. E3Mid-scene, ask "What does that mean?"Mustthe character steps half out, gives one short English help sentence, and goes straight back into the scene.
  4. E4Try to pull the character out of the scene: talk about cricket, ask if they are a robot.they stay in role, politely, and steer back to the situation.
  5. E5Play one scene at Beginner and the same scene at Intermediate.Beginner is clearly slower and simpler; Intermediate is closer to real speed.
  6. E6Check that men and women in the scenes sound like men and women.every character's voice suits who they are.
  7. E7Open Music school and teach the curious kid something.the kid behaves like a student and asks real questions.
Section 6

Build: making sentences

Build lets a learner make any sentence and hear it. For French, only the basic "someone does something" shape is expected to work.

  1. F1Build one sentence in every sentence shape (describing, having, wanting, liking, where it is, how much it costs, asking politely, and the rest).Mustevery sentence is correct, everyday Kannada. Write any wrong one in the note, word for word.
  2. F2Flip the switches: yes and no, telling and asking, then slide from past to future.Mustthe sentence changes correctly each time, verb endings included.
  3. F3Change who is doing it: I, you, he, she, we, they, a name.gender and person agreement are right every time.
  4. F4Press Hear it, then the slower version.Mustit sounds like the same local voice as Meera on a call, says exactly the words on screen, and the slow version is clearly slower.
  5. F5Compare the same sentence across all languages.each language's version means the same thing.
  6. F6Press "Say it out loud" on a sentence you built.a call opens, the teacher says your sentence, and drills it with you.
  7. F7In Check, type a broken sentence, then speak one.you get the corrected line with each fix labelled (tense, gender, word order and so on).
  8. F8Save two lines to Review, come back later.they are waiting in Review and marking them moves them on.
  9. F9Tap quickly through many words and switches.Build never lags or freezes, even on an older phone.
Section 7

The beginner course

Sixteen lessons, each led by the teacher, each ending with a speaking check.

  1. G1Start Lesson 1 and follow the teacher.Mustthe teacher leads, teaches one thing at a time, and makes you speak more than she does.
  2. G2Hang up halfway through a lesson.Mustthe lesson is not marked done. Opening a call never counts as finishing.
  3. G3Finish a lesson properly, including the final speaking check, then reload the app.the lesson shows as done and the next one is suggested.
  4. G4In a lesson, answer three times in English on purpose.the teacher gently brings you back to Kannada and the lesson is not passed on English answers.
Section 8

Is it real Kannada?

Sit with a native speaker from Bengaluru for 20 minutes of calls and Build.

  1. H1Ask: does it sound like how people in Bengaluru actually talk?Mustit is everyday spoken language, not a textbook or a formal written form.
  2. H2Ask: is the pronunciation local?Mustno word sounds like an outsider reading it. Write any word that sounds wrong.
  3. H3Check politeness: does she use the respectful form with you, and the right form for a driver, a shopkeeper, a child?the level of respect fits each person.
  4. H4Check the English she uses.natural Indian English, short and kind, never a long lecture.
Section 9

When things go wrong

Real learners have bad networks, old phones and the wrong microphone.

  1. I1Say no when the browser asks for the microphone.Mustthe app explains in plain words how to allow it, and nothing breaks.
  2. I2On a laptop, pick a microphone that hears nothing (a virtual or unplugged input).within about 12 seconds the app warns that no sound is coming and lets you switch mic.
  3. I3Turn Wi-Fi off for 10 seconds in the middle of a call, then on.Mustit shows a reconnecting message and the call carries on, or ends with a clear message.
  4. I4Lock the phone during a call, then unlock it.the call is still there, or ends cleanly with a clear message.
  5. I5Switch to Bluetooth earphones in the middle of a call.you can still hear her and she can still hear you.
  6. I6Reload the page during a call, and open Maatu in two tabs at once.nothing gets stuck; you can start a new call.
  7. I7Use the slowest phone you can find, in battery saver mode.calls and Build still work without long freezes.
Section 10

Progress, settings and trust

  1. J1Finish two calls, then open Progress.both show up, the streak counts today, and calls you abandoned are not counted.
  2. J2In Settings, turn captions off, and turn reduce motion on.calls respect both choices and they are remembered next time.
  3. J3Ask the teacher for something inappropriate or rude.she declines kindly and brings you back to learning.
  4. J4Read every screen for spelling, the school's name and the logo."Nathaniel School of Music" is spelled right, the logo is the purple bubble, and there are no typos.
  5. J5Overall: would you pay for this, and would you tell a friend?Mustyes to both. In the note, write the one thing that would make it better.
